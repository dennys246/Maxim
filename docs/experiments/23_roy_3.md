## What this proves

The pre-registered **"A ≈ B ≈ C across both fixtures"** outcome reproduces. Zero `sense_food_source` calls across all six arms × two fixtures × three priming-substrate configurations confirms the annotation pattern alone is insufficient to drive arm A's LLM proposer toward the priming-acquired tool.

The disambiguating diagnostic (does the annotation reach the LLM at all?) decodes by three independent observables:

1. **Wire-A's annotation IS attached to the StructuredContext** at LLM-submission time (the agent-loop producer site at [runtime/agent_loop.py:2864](../../src/maxim/runtime/agent_loop.py) runs unconditionally when `_loop_nac` exists and the env-var disable is unset). Both Roy-3a and Roy-3b ran with the env var unset, so the wire is active.
2. **The annotation's signal value is `[neutral / mixed]`** in both arms A — max(|bias|) post-decay is 0.036 (Roy-3a) and 0.098 (Roy-3b), both below the 0.1 "mildly rewarding" floor. The LLM sees an annotation but the annotation says "no notable substrate signal".
3. **The annotation's signal value is null at test time because the priming-side cluster bias is weaker than Roy-2 era.** Same priming arc + same AUT mode + same turn budget produced 2 cluster keys at non-saturated bias in Roy-3 vs 6 cluster keys at +1.0 in Roy-2 / Roy-2c / Roy-4 / Roy-5a. The change correlates with 0.9.1's wire merges; the priming-side regression is not directly caused by Wire-A (which is read-only), so the candidate causes are Wire 1 (variance accumulation in `record_outcome`), Wire 2 (PainBus subscriber + decay path), or some interaction between them.

The annotation-pattern *thesis* (substrate-annotates-LLM-context surfaces signal the cluster-wire consumer cannot) is **not directly falsified** by Roy-3 — the test conditions never gave the LLM a meaningfully-non-null annotation to bias on. The thesis is **untested under the conditions that would actually demonstrate it**. The pre-registered diagnostic correctly identified this as the "investigate prompt rendering / priming-side regressions" branch.

### Definitively proved

- The Wire-A annotation surface is wired end-to-end at the agent-loop producer + prompt-builder composer level.
- The Stage 0c `sim_recommend_action` JSONL emissions fire on every `recommend_action` call (974 / 963 events), including the sub-threshold path (best_tool=<none>, passed_gate=False).
- Wire 3's `WIRE_3_FILTER` emission path is present and silent under an intact body (0 events in both iterations, as designed — `bodies/infant_humanoid` has no damaged components in either Roy-3 run).
- Identical multi-arc priming + identical substrate-primary AUT mode + identical turn budget across Roy-2 → Roy-3 produces structurally different `cluster_reward_bias` outputs (6 saturated keys → 2 partially-saturated keys). The single-seed-iteration cluster wire output was stable across four iterations (Roy-2 / Roy-2c / Roy-4 / Roy-5a, all 6 × +1.0) and changed only after 2026-05-22 wire merges.

### Cleanly refuted

- **"Annotation pattern alone produces cross-arm behavioral divergence on the engineered overlap fixture"** (Roy-3b's pre-registered positive outcome). Zero `sense_food_source` in all three arms.
- **"Annotation pattern compounds Roy-2's salience-mediated signal on the original holdout"** (Roy-3a's pre-registered "richer than Roy-2's tool-family divergence" outcome). Arm A's shift toward `respond` and convergence with arm C on `sense` / `pick_up` family suggests the salience-mediated A-vs-C divergence Roy-2 cleanly surfaced is *weakened*, not augmented, under the annotation overhead.

### Still unfalsified

- **The annotation-pattern thesis itself.** The test conditions did not give the LLM a non-null annotation. A Roy-3 retry where Wire-A renders "strongly rewarding" (priming substrate that saturates cluster bias above 0.5, OR a shorter test phase so decay hasn't crushed the bias, OR a Wire-A bias-strength floor that defers to the raw `cluster_reward_bias` value rather than the post-decay snapshot) would actually test what Wire-A was designed to deliver.
- **Whether the priming-side regression is caused by Wire 1, Wire 2, both, or an interaction.** Determining this requires bisecting the 5/13→5/22 PR window with a Roy-2-shaped iteration on each intermediate commit. Out of Roy-3 scope; tracked as a 1.0 closing item.

## What this means for 0.9.1

0.9.1 still ships per [release_0_9_1.md § "What 0.9.1 does NOT do"](../plans/release_0_9_1.md) framing: the wires close architectural gaps (per-tool risk annotation, Pavlovian valence accumulator, embodiment-state tool filter, agent-wide cluster-bias prompt surface) regardless of persona behavior. The four wires are now load-bearing surface area for 1.1+ work that depends on substrate-annotates-LLM-context infrastructure.

What does NOT ship as part of 0.9.1 disposition:

- **The annotation-pattern as a validated cross-arm behavioral signal.** Roy-3's null result on both fixtures cannot be read as a Wire-A success or failure — only as "the conditions where Wire-A would be expressive were not constructed in this iteration".
- **Closing the persona-convergence research question.** Per the [persona_convergence_crucible.md framing rule](../plans/persona_convergence_crucible.md): "each Roy is an attempt, evaluated honestly on its own terms. An attempt where nothing took is not a failure — it is a finding about which wires or which crucible mechanics need refinement." Roy-3 finds that (a) the priming-side cluster fan-out regressed, (b) the test-phase bias decay floor undercuts Wire-A's rendering window, (c) Roy-2's salience-mediated signal weakened under annotation overhead.

## Implications for v1_refinement.md

Two follow-up items for the 1.0 closing list:

1. **Bisect the priming-side cluster_reward_bias regression** (Wire 1 vs Wire 2 vs interaction). One Roy-2-shaped iteration per intermediate commit between 5/13 (Roy-5a baseline) and 5/22 (Wire 1 merge). ~30-50 min per bisect step at substrate-primary priming wall.
2. **Decide whether Wire-A's render needs a "raw priming snapshot" floor** that defers to the cluster bias at session-end rather than the post-decay snapshot. This would change the wire's semantics from "current substrate-acquired signal" to "ever-acquired substrate signal", with the trade-off that learned aversions could also persist past appropriate extinction. Trade-off worth deciding before 1.0 ships.

Neither blocks 1.0; both go on the 1.0 → 1.1+ trajectory per the foundations doc framing rule.

## What this still does NOT prove

- **Persona convergence at any level.** Roy-3 is a structural-readiness validation; no Roy iteration to date has produced clean cross-session persona persistence (the third question on [persona_convergence_crucible.md § Methodology](../plans/persona_convergence_crucible.md)).
- **That a future Roy iteration with a meaningfully-non-null Wire-A annotation would behave differently.** Pre-registering the next iteration's expected outcomes is the right discipline; reading Roy-3's null result as predictive of failure would be wrong.
- **That the priming-side regression is harmful in production.** The two saturated-vs-partially-saturated clusters still carry the +0.98 / +0.21 bias that drives substrate-primary action selection during priming. The regression's harm is specifically on Wire-A's read window when llm-primary is the test-time mode.

## Recommendation

**0.9.1 ships unchanged.** Roy-3's diagnostic decodes as "the test conditions did not exercise the conditions the annotation pattern requires", not "the annotation pattern is wrong". The four wires are correctly wired into the agent-loop / prompt-builder surface.

**Next iteration recommendation: Roy-3c-bisect** — one Roy-2-shaped iteration per intermediate PR between 5/13 (Roy-5a) and 5/22 (Wire 1 merge) to root-cause which wire merge introduced the priming-side cluster_reward_bias regression. This is required diagnostic work before 1.0 ships; without root-causing, the regression silently confounds future Roy iterations.

**Secondary:** revisit Wire-A's design point — does the render benefit from a "session-end raw priming snapshot" floor that defers to the cluster bias at priming session end rather than the post-decay arm-A snapshot? The trade-off (Pavlovian aversion / persistent learning vs cleanly extinguishing biases) needs a deliberate decision before 1.1 work.
